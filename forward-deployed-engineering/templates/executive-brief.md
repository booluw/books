# Executive Brief: <Customer>: <Engagement name>

*Date: <YYYY-MM-DD> · For: <sponsor and executive audience> · From: <FDE lead / engagement lead> · Period covered: <dates>*

## How to use this template

- **Purpose:** a one-page brief that lets a busy executive understand the situation, the results and what is needed from them in two minutes. It follows the **pyramid principle** (Barbara Minto): state the answer first, then the few arguments that support it, then the detail. Executives read top-down and stop early; put what matters at the top.
- **When:** at phase exits (end of discovery, go / no-go after proof of value, production launch, handover), before steering meetings, and whenever the sponsor needs to brief their own leadership. See the [engagement lifecycle](../chapters/04-engagement-lifecycle.md).
- **Owner:** the lead FDE or engagement lead drafts it; the account executive reviews commercial points.
- **Sign-off:** review with the customer's day-to-day owner before it goes to the sponsor. Numbers on business impact should be ones the customer's finance or operations team accepts.

Background reading: [Chapter 8](../chapters/08-communication.md) and [Chapter 13](../chapters/13-evaluation-and-impact.md).

*Rules: one page. Business terms, not technical ones ("adjusters save 3 hours a week", not "retrieval precision rose to 0.87"). Every number has a baseline and a source. No surprises: if the news is bad, the sponsor hears it in person before reading it. Write it so the executive can forward it to their own boss unchanged.*

## Example (filled)

**Executive Brief: Woodgrove Bank: Complaint Letter Drafting Pilot**

*Date: 2026-11-27 · For: Chief Operating Officer · From: <FDE lead>*

**The answer:** The six-week pilot met two of three targets. Complaint handlers in the Leeds team now draft final-response letters in 18 minutes instead of 41, with no increase in quality-assurance failures. We recommend extending to the Cardiff team in January and fixing the third target (handler adoption) first; this needs one decision from you, below.

**Situation**

- Complaint handling misses its internal eight-week response target on about 12% of cases, driven largely by letter-drafting time.
- We agreed to prove that AI-assisted drafting, with handlers reviewing every letter, could halve drafting time without reducing quality.

**Results**

| Success criterion | Baseline | Target | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median drafting time per letter | 41 min | ≤ 20 min | 18 min | Met |
| QA failure rate on sampled letters | 6.0% | ≤ 6.0% | 5.2% | Met |
| Handlers using the tool weekly | n/a | ≥ 80% | 58% | Not met |

**What this means:** at Leeds volumes, the time saved equals about 3.5 full-time handlers (Operations Finance validated the method). Adoption is the gap: experienced handlers trust their own templates more. We are pairing each with a champion and adding their templates to the tool.

**What's next:** adoption fixes in Leeds (December); Cardiff rollout (January); production security review completes 12 December.

**Ask:** confirm by 5 December that Cardiff's team manager can release four handlers for two half-day training sessions in early January.

**Risk:** the production security review slips past 12 December; mitigation: daily stand-up with the reviewing team, owned by Woodgrove's Head of Information Security.
