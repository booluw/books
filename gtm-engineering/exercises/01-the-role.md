# Exercises: Chapter 1, The Role

## Questions

1. In one paragraph, explain to a software engineer what a GTM engineer does and why companies hire one.
2. Classify each task as mostly RevOps (operate), GTM engineering (build), sales engineering, or growth engineering:
   a. Build a pipeline that detects champion job changes and creates tasks for AEs.
   b. Redesign quota and territory plans for next year.
   c. Run a technical demo and security questionnaire for a prospect.
   d. A/B test the in-product onboarding checklist.
   e. Build an AI research step that answers "does this company run its own fleet?" for 40,000 accounts.
3. List three reasons why "more automated emails" can make GTM results worse.
4. Rate yourself on the skills map (§1.7). Pick the two weakest rows and write one concrete action for each.
5. A job posting says "GTM Engineer: own Clay tables, HubSpot workflows, and outbound infrastructure; SQL a plus." Which chapters of this book matter most for it?

## Solutions

1. A GTM engineer builds the systems that find the right buyers, reach them at the right time with relevant messages, route interested buyers to the right seller fast, and measure what works: data pipelines, enrichment, signals, scoring, routing, outbound and inbound automation, and AI agents. Companies hire one to create pipeline more efficiently than adding headcount.
2. a: GTM engineering. b: RevOps / sales ops. c: Sales engineering. d: Growth engineering. e: GTM engineering.
3. Domain reputation damage and spam-foldering (Chapter 11); annoyed prospects and brand damage in a small market; worse data quality amplified (wrong people, errors); legal and compliance exposure; drowning sellers in low-quality replies.
4. Personal answer.
5. Chapters 5–7 (data model, toolkit, enrichment), 9 (routing), 10–11 (outbound and deliverability), 14 (CRM engineering), 16 (compliance), and 6 for SQL.
