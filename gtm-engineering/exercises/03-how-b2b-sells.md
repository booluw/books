# Exercises: Chapter 3, How B2B Sells

## Questions

1. Define MQL, PQL, SAL, SQL and opportunity in one line each.
2. Why should conversion rates be computed by cohort? Give an example where period ratios mislead.
3. Reverse funnel: target $1.8M new ARR; ACV $24k; win rate 20%; meeting → opp 40%; positive reply → meeting 60%; positive reply rate 1.5%. How many meetings and delivered emails are needed per month (12 months)? Verify with `examples/funnel-metrics`.
4. A company has $4.5M open pipeline for next quarter against a $1.5M target. What is the coverage? Is it healthy?
5. CAC = $18,000; new MRR per customer $1,500; gross margin 80%. What is the CAC payback?
6. Which motion(s) fit: (a) $40/month developer tool; (b) $250k/year supply-chain platform for Fortune 500; (c) $20k/year HR tool for mid-sized companies?

## Solutions

1. MQL: marketing judges the lead ready for sales (fit + engagement). PQL: product usage indicates buying potential. SAL: sales accepted the lead. SQL: sales confirmed real opportunity potential. Opportunity: a tracked potential deal with amount, stage and close date.
2. Deals take time to convert. If March had a lead spike, March's leads may become opportunities in April and May; a March ratio of March opportunities ÷ March leads understates conversion, while April's ratio overstates it.
3. Customers 75 → opps 375 → meetings 938 (≈ 78.2/month) → positive replies 1,564 → delivered ≈ 104,267 (≈ 8,689/month).
4. 3× coverage: within the common 3–4× rule of thumb; depends on win rates and pipeline quality.
5. 18,000 ÷ (1,500 × 0.8) = 15 months.
6. (a) PLG / self-serve, community-led; (b) account-based enterprise sales with partners; (c) inbound + outbound with SDRs/AEs, maybe product-led sales.
